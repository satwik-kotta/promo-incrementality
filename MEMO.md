# Memo: Most of our deals lose money, and the mailer is the reason the rest work

**To:** Category Manager, Grocery
**From:** Satwik Kotta, Analytics
**Re:** Return on price promotions, 2017 (84.51° Complete Journey panel, 2,469 households)

## Bottom line

We ran 5,143 deals on 994 products. **Only 4% made money at a 30% gross margin before vendor funding.** Counting the extra spending deals bring on other items, that rises to 28%, and deals as a whole still lost money. The deals that come closest to paying back are the ones in the weekly mailer.

## What is happening

1. **We discount a lot of sales we would have made anyway.** 41% of units sold on deal would have sold at the regular price that week.
2. **Part of the lift comes from our own shelf.** About 23% of the extra units were taken from other products in the same category.
3. **Unadvertised price cuts are the worst deals.** 61% of their units would have sold anyway, and the median one needs a 96% margin to break even. Mailer deals add 46% more units on top of the price cut and break even at 63%.
4. **The basket helps, but not enough.** Each deal on an item a household buys regularly adds about $0.23 of spending on other items and a small rise in trips that week.
5. **Deal buyers do not stick.** New buyers who found a product on deal were 22% less likely to buy it again at full price within 8 weeks.
6. **Two common rules do not hold here.** There is no sales dip after a deal, and lift does not fade over a 2 or 3 week deal.

## Recommendations

1. **Stop unadvertised price cuts** except where the vendor funds most of the discount.
2. **Put vendor funding in the deal approval.** At a 30% margin, the median deal breaks even only if the manufacturer funds about 62% of the discount.
3. **Review deals on category and basket profit,** not item units. Report incremental units, cannibalized units and the break even margin for every deal.
4. **Do not shorten good deals** on the assumption that the lift fades.
5. **Do not count deal buyers as acquired customers** in loyalty or retention targets.

## Caveats

Margins and vendor funding are not in the data, so results are shown across margins from 20% to 40%. The data is a household panel, not total store sales. The halo is measured in the same week and could be larger over time.
